Production · American Express

Fraud-Detection Virtual Assistant

Agentic triage of high-risk fraud alerts — the assistant builds the case brief, the analyst makes the call.

Problem

Fraud analysts faced a flood of high-risk alerts, each demanding manual investigation across transaction history, customer profile, and prior cases. Reviews were slow, inconsistent, and false positives burned analyst hours that should have gone to real threats.

Constraints

  • Regulated bank: every assisted decision must be explainable and auditable.
  • The analyst stays the decision-maker — the system must never auto-action a fraud call.
  • Customer PII in scope: strict handling and access controls throughout.
  • Fast enough for a live triage workflow — analysts won't wait on a slow assistant.

Architecture

Key decisions & tradeoffs

  • Agentic triage over pure rules. Rules are brittle against novel fraud patterns; an agent with tool use adapts — but every agent action runs inside guardrails, and the reasoning trace is logged for audit.
  • Human-in-the-loop by design. The assistant drafts the case brief; the analyst decides. This cost us full automation, and it was the right call for a regulated bank.
  • Similar-case retrieval. A vector database of historical cases grounds each brief in precedent instead of letting the model free-associate.
  • Measured provider choice. LLM providers and vector databases were benchmarked on latency, cost, and accuracy before committing — the platform's build-vs-buy calls came out of those experiments.

Results

Deployed in production at American Express. Alert triage automated end to end, cutting manual investigation time for analyst teams; XGBoost anomaly-detection models with rule-based logic reduced false positives and manual review effort. Decision-support tooling now assists analysts on high-risk transaction reviews and customer risk assessments.

Production · Vanguard

Enterprise Document-Intelligence RAG Assistant

Retrieval-augmented Q&A over fund and account documentation — every answer cited, every claim traceable.

Problem

Compliance and support teams spent hours hunting answers across sprawling fund and account documentation. Keyword search missed context, and slow lookups bottlenecked review cycles.

Constraints

  • Compliance setting: every answer must be cited and traceable to source documents.
  • Legacy documentation in varied formats — ingestion had to be robust, not demo-clean.
  • API-first access: downstream systems needed programmatic access to historical records.
  • SEC/FINRA-adjacent regulatory expectations on the review process.

Architecture

Key decisions & tradeoffs

  • Citations as a hard requirement. No uncited claims, ever — this ruled out several "helpful but loose" generation strategies and shaped the whole prompt design.
  • Evaluated vector stores before choosing. Pinecone, FAISS, OpenSearch, and pgvector were compared on latency, cost, and accuracy for our workload instead of defaulting to the familiar option.
  • Chunking tuned, not defaulted. Chunk size and overlap were engineered around precision/recall on our docs; small chunks won on precision, larger ones on context — the tradeoff is documented per document type.
  • LLM-as-a-judge in the loop. Automated evals scored faithfulness and relevance on every iteration, so regressions were caught before humans saw them.

Results

Deployed in production at Vanguard. Cited, context-aware answers cut lookup time for compliance and support teams, with API-first access to historical records. A companion pipeline generates 2-page automated summaries of fund disclosures and account statements, cutting manual document-review effort for operations teams.

Production · Vanguard

Fine-Tuned Financial-Domain LLM

LoRA/QLoRA fine-tuning of open models for compliance-heavy workflows — where prompting alone hit a ceiling.

Problem

Base open-source models hallucinated on compliance-heavy financial workflows and missed domain terminology. Prompt engineering improved things but plateaued — the models needed domain knowledge baked in, not bolted on.

Constraints

  • Limited curated domain data — every training example had to earn its place.
  • Training budget: full fine-tuning of large models was off the table.
  • No regression on general capability — the model still had to handle everything else.
  • Everything through responsible-AI review before production.

Architecture

Key decisions & tradeoffs

  • LoRA/QLoRA over full fine-tuning. A fraction of the compute, and adapters stay swappable per domain — full fine-tuning would have been slower, pricier, and harder to version.
  • Open models (Llama, Mistral). Full control over weights and data privacy — non-negotiable for financial data.
  • Eval set before training. Hallucination probes and compliance-accuracy tests were built first, so "better" was measured rather than felt. Training stopped when the evals said so.
  • Fine-tuning + hybrid prompting, not either/or. Prompting handled structure and style; the adapter handled domain knowledge. Each did what it was best at.

Results

Shipped through Vanguard's responsible-AI review process. The fine-tuned models measurably reduced hallucinations and improved compliance accuracy across investment and brokerage workflows, where the base models had plateaued.
